The Long Road

Turn 65, and the rest of your life runs along one of a few health paths: staying well, living with chronic illness, needing help with daily tasks, needing heavy help at home, or living in a nursing home. Each path costs Medicare and costs you differently. Pick where you start and see how long the road tends to run, what it costs, how bad the worst stretches get, how often it ends on Medicaid, and what happens if Medicare's hospital fund runs short in 2033.

2. What the road costs

Out-of-pocket care costs include hospital, nursing-home, doctor, dental, drug and home-care spending, and the last year of life. They exclude Medigap and Medicare Advantage premiums, which are shown nowhere on this page, and anything Medicaid pays. Medicare spending leaves out nursing-facility, hospice and institutional care, which the survey behind it does not cover, so it is a lower bound. Spending is persistent from year to year, as the survey shows, which is what makes the worst cases as heavy as they are.

4. If the hospital fund runs short

A shortfall reaches households only through a channel: thinner supplemental or Medicare Advantage benefits, higher cost sharing, or care that is not delivered and has to be bought. The scenarios assume a share of the gap reaches people who are not on Medicaid; for people on Medicaid, it pays Medicare cost sharing.
